# Day 8 Reflection

**The most difficult concept and how I overcame it.** The hardest concept was concurrency: understanding how two people can both "see" a seat as available and both try to buy it. At first I assumed that checking the seat status in my code before updating it was enough. It took working through a timeline of two requests, step by step, to see the gap between the check and the update. I overcame it by drawing those timelines and by learning that the fix is to let the database do the check and the change as one atomic operation, with a unique constraint as a safety net. Writing the double-booking section of TicketHub forced me to explain this in my own words, which made it stick.

**What I would improve in my capstone.** Based on the feedback I received, my estimates were too optimistic and my failure handling was too thin. I would state my assumptions more clearly (requests per user, traffic peaks) and show how the numbers change if they are wrong. I would also add more detail on what happens when something fails halfway, such as a payment that succeeds right when a hold expires, and add monitoring and alerts so problems are noticed quickly. I would also test my design with a small load test instead of only reasoning about it.

**What I will learn next.** I want to learn how databases really work under load: isolation levels, locking and indexing. After that I want to study distributed systems topics such as replication, sharding and message queues in depth, and to build a small working version of a booking system so I can measure performance and see the design break and recover, rather than only describing it.
